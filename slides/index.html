PSA turns free broadcast video plus open play-by-play data into a per-outing score of how each pitcher compares with his own recent outings. Built for schools and teams with no budget for tracking hardware.

The problem

In a tournament, a high-school pitcher may throw on back-to-back days; the rest rules count pitches, not changes in his speed or delivery

Pro clubs

Statcast, Hawk-Eye and TrackMan measure every pitch: speed, spin, release point.

Schools and amateur teams

Pitch counts by hand. No budget for tracking systems or paid data.

How it works

Five steps, about an hour per full game on a Mac

01

Find the pitching shot

Centre-field frames kept; replays and close-ups skipped.

02

Read the scorebug

Count, pitch count and speed, by macOS text recognition.

03

Track the pose

Apple Vision body pose; a learned picker finds the release frame.

04

Join each pitch

Matched to open play-by-play where it exists; otherwise the scorebug's count changes give the result.

05

Score the outing

PSI: this outing against his own previous three.

PSA · 05

Each step runs locally on a Mac with built-in Apple frameworks. The demo page walks the 2025 黑豹旗 final through all five steps.

The score

PSI: is he pitching like himself?

d = |change| ÷ threshold

Threshold: the smallest real change at 95% (pitch sampling, plus game-to-game variation between games). d = 1 is a change just big enough to be real.

Each part separately (velocity, command, posture, timing, consistency); the baseline is the previous three outings (between games) or the first half of the same game (in-game score).

part score = 100 · e^(−d²/4)

100 with no change, 78 at the threshold, 37 at twice it. Equal to 100 × Harrington's two-sided desirability (1965), n = 2, limits at ±2 thresholds.

PSI = ½ results + ½ mechanics

Results: velocity, command. Mechanics: posture, timing, release consistency.

80+ steady

60–79 worth a look

below 60 flagged

PSA · 06

Dividing by the detection threshold puts km/h, degrees and milliseconds on one scale. The bell curve is flat near zero, so noise costs almost nothing, and it has no cliff at the threshold. Each part score equals 100 times Harrington's two-sided desirability function from quality engineering (1965), exp(−|y′|ⁿ), with n = 2 and the specification limits at plus and minus two detection thresholds, where it scores 1/e, our 37.

PSA · 08 · Rebas Open Data 2023–2025, every CPBL outing

Inside one game there is no day-to-day variation, so a sampling-only threshold is right, and the in-game score is calibrated and sensitive. Between games the threshold must include normal variation: with fastball speed's 1.5 km/h added, shuffled outings cross about 2% of the time, as a drop-only part should.

PSA · 12

These checks passed. On high-school video the release picker matched every hand label it answered, and the in-game score does not treat noise inside a game as change. The two angle checks come from CPBL broadcasts.

What we can't claim yet

PSI notices change; it does not yet predict

Result changes rarely reach the threshold

Fastball speed varies 1.5 km/h between games beyond sampling (CPBL 2023–2024). With that in the threshold, false alarms fall to 2%, but only drops beyond about 5 km/h are flagged, so almost no outing reaches the flag level: no 2025 CPBL outing, and none of 20 high-school pairs (was 40%).

Not yet confirmed by coaches or by results

High-school games have no open play-by-play, so only coaches can say whether a PSI flag mattered. In CPBL, a low results score did not predict the next start (ρ = −0.02).

Two tournaments, 16 pitchers

蔡辰瀧 (平鎮) lost 5.6 km/h over four 黑豹旗 games; 洪靖翔 (彰化) threw 7 km/h harder in November than in June's 玉山盃. Leads for coaches, and the reason PSI keeps its baseline within one tournament.

PSA · 13

The velocity threshold now includes normal game-to-game variation, so false alarms are rare; the cost is that the results half almost never flags, so its prediction can't be tested yet. School games have no open play-by-play, so the outcome test has to come from coaches.
