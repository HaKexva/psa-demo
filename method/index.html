PSA · Pitcher Status Analyzer

Pitcher analysis from public game video. For every outing, PSA finds the pitcher's pitches in a broadcast or highlight video, matches each one to the official play-by-play, measures his body at the moment of release, and scores the outing against his own previous outings: PSI, the Pitcher Stability Index (100 = pitching like himself). It uses only free, public and open sources, so a school team with no budget can run it. The same core library is meant to serve any level of play.

  • Live demo: https://hakexva.github.io/psa-demo/ (this document is also published there as the Method page)
  • Built for the 2026 野革盃 Rebas Baseball Data Hackathon
  • Status: working prototype. The measurements are validated; whether PSI predicts outcomes is not yet shown (see Validation and Limits)

1. The question

Is this pitcher pitching like himself today?

A box score says what happened, not whether a pitcher's delivery has drifted. Teams with tracking systems (TrackMan, Hawk-Eye) can see that; most school and amateur teams cannot. But their games are often broadcast (黑豹旗 on 公視+), and every CPBL game has highlights on YouTube and an official play-by-play. PSA turns that public material into a per-outing report: workload, velocity and strikes, and release posture and timing, each compared with the pitcher's own recent outings.

PSA measures change, not quality. A low PSI says something moved; it does not say whether the change is good or bad, or why. It is for workload and consistency tracking, not medical assessment.

Pitch data

  • Rebas Open Data (CPBL 2023-2025, per game / plate appearance / pitch, with pitch type and velocity), ODC-By v1.0, attribution required. Used for 2025: each highlight pitch is matched to its row, which gives its type, speed, runners and result (psa-rebas).
  • CPBL official site play-by-play (2026, which Rebas does not cover yet): per pitch the pitcher's running pitch count, ball-strike count, batter, runners, outs and result, but no pitch type or speed. Speeds then come from the broadcast scorebug, and a fastball is a pitch of 143 km/h or more (in his 2025 Rebas data that keeps 96% of his fastballs and 3% of other pitches). Fetched once per game and cached; raw data is not republished (psa-cpbl).
  • Scorebug: without any play-by-play (amateur games), pitch counts, speeds and counts are read from the broadcast's on-screen graphics alone.

Our own labels

  • Release frames: the project owner marked the exact release frame of 510 pitches on a labeling page (228 of 胡智為's 2025 pitches, 282 from the 黑豹旗 and CPBL farm games). They train and test the release picker.
  • Field landmarks: 13 points per video (batter's-box corners, plate, rubber ends) for the 17 videos of 2025, to solve each camera's direction.

Reference dimensions and rules

  • Field dimensions (batter's boxes 4 x 6 ft, plate 17 in, rubber 60 ft 6 in from the plate's back point, mound 10 in high) from the official rules, via Wikipedia "Batter's box".
  • CTBA 2025 黑豹旗 rule E4.11 (pitch counts and rest) for the tournament workload check.

3. How the pipeline works

One command, psa-report, runs the whole chain for a pitcher's series of games; each step is also its own command. Every step runs locally on a Mac (Apple Vision is part of macOS); one highlight video takes about 4-9 minutes.

video ──> 1 pitching shots ──> 2 scorebug ──> 3 pose ──> 4 release frame ──> 5 match to play-by-play
                                                                               │
report <── 9 pages <── 8 PSI <── 7 camera handling <── 6 measurements <───────┘
  1. Find the pitching shot (shots.py). Broadcasts cut between cameras; only the centre-field view behind the pitcher shows his delivery. Each frame becomes a tiny normalised colour thumbnail (scorebug blanked) and is compared with a reference centre-field frame. The reference is picked per video: candidate frames shortly before scorebug updates are clustered and the first cluster that passes a pose check (pitcher low in the frame, batter and catcher above) wins. It can be pinned by hand when the automatic pick is wrong.
  2. Read the scorebug (scorebug.py, highlights.py). Apple Vision text recognition reads the on-screen graphics about twice a second: batter, pitcher, ball-strike count, the pitcher's running pitch count and the last pitch's speed. Layouts differ by broadcaster and season (2025: "PITCHES 50", "NP 42", "用球數25", "P: 15"; 2026: "10球", "P:31", "NP： | 1", velocity in its own box), so they are parsed by pattern, with per-broadcaster settings in lab/profiles/*.toml.
  3. Track the pitcher's pose (pose.py). Apple Vision body pose runs on every pitching-shot frame; the pitcher is the tracked person low in the frame.
  4. Find the release frame (release_model.py). A gradient-boosting classifier (scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting) scores every frame of a shot on torso-normalised pose features and how they change over the neighbouring frames; the highest score is the release. Held out one whole start at a time, it lands within ±2 frames (±67 ms) of the hand label on 99% of 228 CPBL releases (±1 frame: 98%); a hand-written rule managed 14-51%. Hand labels, where they exist, replace the picker's frame.
  5. Match each pitch to the play-by-play (highlights.py). The scorebug's pitch count before the pitch, the count and the batter pin a video pitch to its exact play-by-play row. Highlights run in game order, so matched neighbours bound ambiguous ones; the pitch that ends a plate appearance breaks remaining ties.
  6. Measure the release (pitcher.py, delivery.py). At the release frame, in the image: side lean (trunk tilt to the glove side), forward lean (from the shortening of the torso), shoulder line and hip line angles, arm slot, and the release point (height above the hips and distance to the side, in torso lengths). From the frames before release: hip lead (how much earlier the hips open than the shoulders; hip-shoulder separation shrinks with fatigue, Erickson 2016) and shoulder opening time. A plausibility rule drops misread throwing arms: the wrist must be detected with confidence, sit 1.2-2.6 torso lengths above the hips and 0.5-2.2 out to the side, with the arm nearly straight (shoulder-to-wrist reach ≥ 0.9 torso lengths).
  7. Handle the camera (pitcher.py, landmarks.py). Angles seen from different stadiums are not directly comparable. Three levels, best first:
  8. Field geometry: the 13 landmarks, labeled once per video, give the camera's direction and focal length (least squares on the reprojection error, with a soft prior that the camera is about 400 ft out). Joints are then projected onto the vertical plane through the release point, as if seen from straight behind. For the 2025 cameras this moved angles by only 0.24-0.32° (median): CPBL centre-field cameras sit 4-7° toward third base and 3-4° above the pitcher.
  9. Other pitchers: every other pitcher in the same videos is measured too (415 releases from 63 pitchers), and camera offsets are fitted with a mixed model (value ~ camera + pitcher, random game effect), leaving the pitcher being scored out. The offsets' uncertainty is added to the noise threshold.
  10. Same camera only: compare with earlier outings under the same stadium and broadcast feed.
  11. Score the outing: PSI, below.
  12. Build the pages (hu_study.py, pitcher.py, home.py, publish.py). Static HTML pages: a season report per pitcher, a tournament page, single-game pages, and the landing page, published to GitHub Pages (psa-publish).

4. PSI: the algorithm

PSI asks, per outing: is he pitching like his own baseline (section 5)? 100 means yes.

Step 1, change in noise units. For each part, the change from the baseline is divided by the smallest change the measurement can detect at 95%, so a change at the edge of noise counts as 1:

  • medians of per-pitch values (posture, timing): d = |median_now − median_base| ÷ (1.96 · s · √(1/n_now + 1/n_base)), with s the pitch-to-pitch scatter around each sample's own median, pooled;
  • velocity: on season reports the same with means (speeds are whole km/h, so a median moves in 1 km/h steps; the tournament page uses medians); only drops count;
  • command (strike rate): a two-proportion test, 1.96 · √(p(1−p)(1/n_now + 1/n_base)); only drops count;
  • consistency (release-point spread): ln(spread ratio) ÷ (1.96 × the standard error of both outings' ln spread, by bootstrap resampling of their pitches); only a wider spread counts;
  • across cameras, the camera's uncertainty (offset error, or 0.3° with field geometry) is added to the threshold in quadrature;
  • a part made of several metrics (posture: side lean, forward lean, arm slot, release height and side, shoulder and hip lines) takes the root-mean-square of their d.

Step 2, part score. score = 100 · e^(−d²/4): 100 with no change, 78 at the noise threshold, 37 at twice it.

Step 3, PSI. PSI = ½ · results + ½ · mechanics, where results = mean(velocity, command) and mechanics = mean(posture, timing, consistency). Parts that cannot be measured are left out of their half; if a whole half is missing, PSI is the other half. The 50/50 split is a project decision, not a research result: no study gives weights between results and mechanics (the closest published scores, Dillon 2025 and Ma 2021, are built differently). Both halves and every part are shown on each report.

Reading it. 80+ steady; 60-79 worth a look; below 60 flagged. Reports name what moved (e.g. "side lean +4.8°") only for outings below 80, because smaller raw differences across cameras are mostly camera offset.

Guard against noise. Every tournament run also scores two random halves of the same game against each other. They come from one pitcher on one day, so they should almost never be flagged; more than 5% would mean a part treats noise as change. This test caught a fixed 15% consistency tolerance that flagged 19% of same-game halves; with the bootstrap threshold it is 0% of 630 (see docs/findings/pitfalls.md).

Game score (the outcome PSI is checked against): Bill James's game score with earned runs only: 50 + outs + 2 per full inning after the 4th + strikeouts − 2 · hits − 4 · earned runs − walks.

5. Baselines: what each outing is compared with

This is the authoritative definition; the code is hu_study.py (season reports, BASE_N = 3) and pitcher.stability_index (tournament pages).

Season reports (a rolling baseline: his previous 3 outings). A season has many outings, and a pitcher's delivery can change for good reasons; a rolling baseline follows gradual change and flags sudden change.

Part Compared with
Velocity, command his previous 3 outings (all their pitches)
Posture, timing, consistency his previous 3 outings, using the best camera handling the outing has: field geometry (any camera), else other-pitcher offsets (any camera), else only earlier outings under the same camera
First outing of the series none: it is the baseline and gets no PSI

Starts and relief outings share one baseline, and the window crosses seasons (his first 2026 outing is compared with his last three 2025 starts).

Tournament pages (a fixed baseline). A tournament has 2-5 games per pitcher, too few to roll.

Part Compared with
Velocity, command his first outing of the tournament
Consistency his earliest outing with measured deliveries
Posture, timing the earliest earlier game under the same camera, else the earliest earlier game (camera-corrected)
First outing none: it is the baseline and gets no PSI

6. Validation so far

Check Result
Release frame vs hand label (held out one start at a time) within ±2 frames on 99% of 228 CPBL releases
Same pitch, two broadcast cuts (measurement repeatability) side lean 1.4°, shoulder line 1.4°, arm slot 1.2° (median difference, 67 / 67 / 14 pairs)
Camera direction from field markings cuts of the same feed agree within ~0.1-0.5°; correction to angles 0.24-0.32°
Same-game half-split null test (consistency) 0% of 630 flagged
PSI vs game score, 胡智為 (15 starts, 2025-2026) Spearman ρ = 0.31, p = 0.26: no reliable link yet
Known change PSI caught his first start back from relief (2025-08-08): more side lean (+4.8°), shoulder line +5.1°, fastball +3-4 km/h, 0 earned runs. A real change, and a good outing: PSI flags change, not quality

What is established: the inputs are measured consistently and PSI notices delivery changes. What is not: that a low PSI predicts a worse outing, an injury or fatigue. Planned tests: a league-wide results check on Rebas 2023-2025 (thousands of outings, velocity and command parts), PSI at known-change events (role changes, returns from injury) against ordinary outings, within-game drift with pitch count on full broadcasts, and coach review of flagged outings.

Noise we once mistook for signal, and the guard for each, is listed in docs/findings/pitfalls.md. Detailed findings: docs/findings/.

7. Limits

  • Highlights, not full games. Highlight videos show 10-25 of a starter's pitches, 92% of them the last pitch of a plate appearance. Full-game video would give 60-90 pitches per start and remove that bias.
  • Low power for consistency. With 15-25 pitches a spread estimate is noisy: even a tripled spread usually scores 50-70, not below 60.
  • 2D video. Angles are measured in the image (or on one plane with field geometry). Single-camera 2D measurement is less accurate than motion capture (Dobos 2025, Dunlevy 2005), which is why PSI only compares a pitcher with himself under known camera conditions.
  • Small samples. One CPBL pitcher over two seasons and one high-school team: enough to build and check the method, not to prove PSI's value.
  • 2026 data. The CPBL play-by-play has no pitch types or speeds; speeds come from the scorebug and fastballs are inferred by speed.

8. Code: design and layout

Design principles. Free and local (Apple Vision on a Mac, open-source Python, public data); one command per step so each can be rerun and checked; every source logged in docs/SOURCES.md; numbers and page text generated from the same report files; measurements always relative to the pitcher's own baseline; noise thresholds measured, never assumed (checked by a null test and unit tests).

lab/
  src/psa_lab/        the package (commands below)
  profiles/*.toml     per-broadcaster scorebug and framing settings
  models/             the learned release picker
  data/series/*.json  series definitions (pitcher, games, videos, data source per outing)
  data/label/**.json  hand labels (release frames, field landmarks)
  tests/              pytest suite (~30 s, no video needed)
  web/                Tailwind config for the landing page
docs/                 research notes, findings, SOURCES.md
Command Module What it does
psa-report report.py one command: videos → pitch data → scan → PSI → page, for a series
psa-highlight highlights.py scan a highlight video: shots, scorebug, pose, releases, matched pitches
psa-analyze analyze.py scan a full broadcast game (every pitch)
psa-rebas, psa-cpbl rebas.py, cpbl.py pitch data from Rebas Open Data / the CPBL site
psa-hu-study hu_study.py season study: per-outing PSI, camera handling, correlations, report
psa-pitcher pitcher.py tournament study: workload vs rules, PSI per game, null test
psa-landmarks landmarks.py field-landmark labeling page and camera solve
psa-label label_tool.py release-frame labeling page
psa-train-release, psa-eval, psa-tune release_model.py, evaluate.py, tune.py train and evaluate the release picker
psa-status status.py within-game drift from a pitcher's opening pitches
psa-demo, psa-publish build_demo.py, publish.py build the pages and publish to GitHub Pages

Run: cd lab && uv sync, then for example uv run psa-report data/series/hu.json. Tests: uv run pytest.

Not in this repository: game videos and generated outputs under lab/data/ (tens of GB, re-created by the commands), the Rebas Open Data archive (download CPBL-<year>-OpenData.zip from https://github.com/rebas-tw/rebas.tw-open-data into lab/data/rebas/) and the cached CPBL pages (fetched again by psa-cpbl).
