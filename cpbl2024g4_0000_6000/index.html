PSA · Pitcher Status Analyzer · how the analysis works

投手狀態監測One broadcast video in, one row per pitch out: pitch count, velocity, delivery type and the pitcher's posture at release.

The input is game video from the center-field camera angle, the view every broadcast uses for pitches. The system finds that view on its own, reads the scorebug for pitch count, velocity and runners, and measures the pitcher at release. The question: is it consistent enough to see a pitcher change across an outing?

How it works

Four passes over the video, then one score

1

Find the pitching shot

Each frame is shrunk to 64×36 and compared with a reference center-field frame. Replays, close-ups and dugout shots score low and are skipped.

2

Read the scorebug

macOS text recognition reads the overlay twice a second. After each pitch it shows the velocity, then the pitch count (NP) and ball-strike count update.

Images
3

Track the pitcher's pose

Apple Vision body pose runs on every pitching-shot frame. Release is the frame a learned picker, trained on hand-labeled releases, scores highest in the shot.

4

Join into one row per pitch

Each detected release is matched to the next NP increment on the scorebug, within 8 seconds. Pickoffs and false detections have no NP change, so they drop out. Runners on base mark the pitch as a stretch delivery.

5

Score the outing (PSI)

The rows of one outing are compared with the same pitcher's earlier outings, part by part: velocity and command (results), posture, timing and release consistency (mechanics). Season reports use his previous three outings; tournament reports use his earlier games of the tournament. One game alone has no season score, so the outing board below scores each pitcher's second half against his first half with the same formulas. Season and tournament scores are on the live reports, and the full algorithm with examples is on the home page.

1
d = |change| ÷ threshold

The threshold is the smallest change the measurement can detect at 95%, from pitch-to-pitch scatter and the number of pitches. d = 1 is a change just big enough to be real.

2
score = 100 · e−d²/4

100 with no change, 78 at the threshold, 37 at twice it. Velocity and command count only drops; consistency only a wider spread.

3
PSI = ½ results + ½ mechanics

Each half is the average of its parts.

Outing board

How each pitcher changed through the outing

This is the within-game view: each pitch against the pitcher's own early pitches in the same game. PSI (step 5) compares whole outings instead. With runners on, pitchers switch from the windup to the stretch, which changes timing and posture. Each pitch is tagged from the base diamond on the scorebug, and the baseline uses the pitcher's own first 10 windup pitches so stretch pitches do not read as drift. Velocity drop is the most consistent within-game fatigue sign in the research. Arm slot is shown for reference only: its change under fatigue (about 1.7°) is smaller than video pose error.

Next steps

  1. Widen PSI's thresholds to include ordinary outing-to-outing variation. A league-wide test on every 2023–2025 CPBL outing found fastball speed moves about ±2 km/h between outings, wider than the current threshold assumes, and the results half did not predict the next outing.
  2. Check the mechanics half at known changes (2025 role switches and returns from 30+ days out), in progress.
  3. Run full-game video for whole outings (60 to 90 pitches), so every pitch counts, not only the ones a highlight shows.
  4. Have coaches review the outings PSI flags.
Metrics are for workload and consistency tracking. They are not a medical assessment.
