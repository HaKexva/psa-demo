PSA · Pitcher Status Analyzer · pitchers across a tournament

投手追蹤

Every number here comes from the broadcast video on its own: pitch counts and velocity from the scorebug, release posture from pose at the release frame. Workload is checked against the tournament's pitch-count rule. Posture is reported as change from the pitcher's own baseline game: a change means the mechanics moved, not that the pitcher was tired.

PSI · Pitcher Stability Index (投手穩定指數): 100 means the pitcher looks like his own baseline, within each tournament: his first outing with enough pitches (5+ fastballs, 10+ known results) for velocity and command, his earliest outing with measured deliveries for consistency, and for posture and timing the camera check's baseline game (the earliest earlier game under the same camera, else the earliest earlier game). Half of it is results: velocity (fastball drop) and command (strike-rate drop); the other half is mechanics: posture (release posture, camera-calibrated), timing (hip lead, shoulder opening) and consistency (delivery spread). Parts count equally inside each half. Each part is his change from baseline divided by the smallest change that is real at 95% (d): pitch-sampling noise, plus for velocity the normal game-to-game variation (about 1.5 km/h), scored 100·exp(−d²/4): at that limit a part scores about 78, at twice it about 37. Parts that cannot be measured in a game are left out; if a whole half is missing, PSI is the other half (hover a score for its parts). In-game scores the same way within one game: its second half against its first half, mechanics on his most-used delivery only, with pitch-sampling thresholds (no game-to-game term inside one game). 80+ steady, 60–79 worth a look, below 60 flagged. Release posture

Hip lead: how much earlier the hips turn to face home than the shoulders (ms before release when each reaches 70% of its release width, seen from behind); hip-shoulder separation shrinks with fatigue in adolescents (Erickson 2016). Delivery time: leg-lift peak to release, windup (W) and stretch (S) separately. Consistency: typical distance of the last 0.6 s of the pose path from a reference path, in torso lengths: own = this game's median path, vs base = the baseline game's.
Strike % counts called and swinging strikes, fouls, and pitches that end a plate appearance (balls in play and strikeouts are strikes); a plate appearance that ends on a 3-ball count is left out because a walk and a ball in play look the same on the scorebug. Early and late split the outing at half its pitches. Release point is the throwing hand at release relative to the pitcher's own body, in torso lengths (height above the hips / distance out to the side); spread is the typical distance from the game's median point.
Pitch counts are the highest NP read off the broadcast scorebug, so they are a lower bound when the last pitches of an outing were not read. Posture uses hand-labeled release frames where a game has them, otherwise frames the learned release picker marked usable; poses far from a game's median (the pose model catching someone else) are dropped and counted.
For workload and consistency tracking only. Not a medical assessment.
