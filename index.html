Who uses it, and when

For the coach deciding who pitches tomorrow

High-school and amateur coaches, their pitchers and athletic trainers. No analyst and no tracking hardware: we process the public game video, and the coach opens a report link on a phone.

  1. During a tournament

    The night after a game day

    Each pitcher's report: pitches against the rest rules, speed and delivery against his earlier games, his second half against his first. Then the coach picks tomorrow's pitcher.

  2. Between tournaments

    The same pitcher months later

    玉山盃 in June, 黑豹旗 in November: did his fastball or his delivery move? Leads for the next training block.

  3. In practice · planned

    Live ABs, filmed by a phone

The algorithm

One score per outing: is he pitching like himself?

PSI (Pitcher Stability Index) compares each outing with his own previous three. 100 means nothing moved. The full method (data, pipeline, baselines, validation, references) is on the Method page.

1

Measure the change

d = |change| ÷ threshold

For each part, this outing against his previous three. The threshold is the smallest change that is real at 95%: pitch-to-pitch scatter over the number of pitches, plus the normal variation between games (about 1.5 km/h for fastball speed).

So d counts changes in detectable steps: d = 1 is a change just big enough to be real, whether the part is in km/h, degrees or ms.

2

Score each part

score = 100 · e−d²/4

e ≈ 2.718, the base of natural growth and decay. A bell curve, the shape of the normal distribution.

78 at the threshold37 at twice it
3

Combine into PSI

PSI = ½ results
PSI + ½ mechanics

Each half is the average of its parts. If a half cannot be measured for an outing, PSI is the other half.

80+steady, like his previous outings
60–79worth a look
below 60flagged: something moved
Example · 1 · Measure the change

胡智為, 2025-09-26: his fastball averaged 0.7 km/h below his previous three outings. With 47 fastballs, sampling alone would make 0.53 km/h detectable, but fastball speed also moves about 1.5 km/h from game to game, which raises the threshold to 3.48 km/h.

threshold = 1.96 · √(0.27² + 1.52² · 4/3) = 3.48

0.27 km/hSampling: the noise in the gap between today's average fastball and the baseline's, from pitch-to-pitch scatter and the number of fastballs. More pitches make it smaller.
1.52 km/hGame-to-game variation (τ): how much a pitcher's true fastball level moves between games beyond sampling, measured on CPBL pitchers' consecutive outings, 2023–2024.
4/3 = 1 + 1/3Today's game brings one share of that variation; the baseline averages his previous three outings, so it brings a third.
√The two sources are independent, so their squares add; the root turns the total back into km/h: 1.78 km/h, the typical size of a change when nothing really changed.
1.96The 95% point of the normal distribution: a chance change goes past 1.96 times that size only 5% of the time.

Game-to-game variation (3.08) is about 40 times the sampling term (0.07), so more pitches barely move the threshold: before it was added, the threshold was 1.96 × 0.27 = 0.53 km/h.

d = 0.7 ÷ 3.48 = 0.20

A fifth of a normal game-to-game swing. Step 2 turns it into a score.

Why this curve

01

One scale

Dividing by the threshold removes units, so speed, angles and timing can be averaged.

02

Flat near zero

Changes inside the noise cost almost nothing: half a threshold still scores 94.

03

No cliff

A pass/fail rule would call d = 0.99 and 1.01 opposites. The curve falls smoothly, faster for bigger changes.

04

The 4 sets the bands

At the threshold a part scores 78, just short of steady. Below 60 needs d ≥ 1.43, which noise alone reaches about 0.5% of the time.

e itself is a fixed constant, not a tuned value. The formula was chosen for this project, and it turns out to be a special case of an established one: Harrington's two-sided desirability function from quality engineering (1965), exp(−|y′|ⁿ), with n = 2 and its limits at twice the threshold, where both give 1/e ≈ 37. The curve and the 4 are not fitted to outcomes; the threshold underneath is what has to be right (see Method · Validation).

Two scores, never mixed

Inside one game there is no day-to-day variation, so the in-game score can use a tight threshold. Between games the threshold must also allow for normal game-to-game variation.

Where it stands

The measurement holds. The prediction is not proven yet.

What holds

  • 114 / 114 automatic release picks within ±1 frame of hand labels, on a 黑豹旗 camera the picker had never seen
  • 1.4° median side-lean difference for the same pitch in two CPBL broadcast cuts
  • 0.1–0.5° camera direction agreement between cuts of one CPBL feed
  • 0.2% of random halves flagged in 78 玉山盃 and 黑豹旗 outings: noise is not read as change

What we can't claim yet

  • A low results score did not predict the next start, across 1,563 CPBL starts (ρ = −0.02).
  • Between games, fastball speed varies about 1.5 km/h beyond sampling. With that in the threshold, false alarms fall to 2%, but only drops beyond about 5 km/h are flagged, so the results half rarely flags. On the 17 high-school games, 40% of game-to-game fastball changes crossed the old threshold; with that variation included, none of the 20 pairs does.
  • Not yet confirmed by coaches: high-school games have no open play-by-play, so only coaches can say whether a flag mattered.
  • Leads, not verdicts: 蔡辰瀧 (平鎮) lost 5.6 km/h over four 黑豹旗 games; 洪靖翔 (彰化) threw 7 km/h harder in November than in June's 玉山盃.
  • With field landmarks on all 40 known-change videos, the mechanics half still reaches only 3 of 14 event outings: highlights barely show relievers. Full games are needed.

Plan: the 2026 玉山盃 (21 full games) is being processed now; during the 2026 黑豹旗 (10/25–11/29) we produce reports for selected teams the morning after each game day; at the 12/12 final, this year's reports and the first coach ratings of the flags; after it, game-to-game thresholds for the mechanics parts and a practice-mode trial with one school team. What you need

  • Game video from one fixed camera behind center field, such as a free tournament stream
  • A Mac (Apple Vision, included with macOS)
  • Pitch-by-pitch data when it exists; otherwise the scorebug

Limits

  • The camera angle must match from game to game, or field landmarks must be labeled to correct it
  • Highlights show 10–25 pitches per start; full games show all of them
  • The score flags change, not quality
  • Not a medical assessment
